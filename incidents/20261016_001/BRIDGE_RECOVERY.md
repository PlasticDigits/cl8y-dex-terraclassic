## Blacklist protection and its limit

The deployed bridge blacklist is `0xdc1230a407903bcbbffa85e1e62879301a95e714`. It marks both reported BSC attacker wallets, `0x46b2ebf5ec9bbbca91fb826a6c13aa7ccdb66f8d` and `0x377a56e0fea12d5e0632429326b5cd9e47e10e5b`, as blacklisted. GuardBridge `0x12fedd29e71f66157e985aa1aaae434253e39a22` registers it in both its **deposit** and **withdrawal** module sets. The blacklist authority is the existing access manager `0xa958d75c61227606df21e3261ba80dc399d19676`; no ownership transfer or bridge implementation upgrade is established by these response transactions.

At the pre-unpause verification snapshot (block **127900715**, **02:44:55 UTC**), the bridge remained paused, both registrations and blacklist entries were present, and read-only full-guard calls for vFDUSD and USDT rejected both attacker wallets with the expected `Blacklisted(address)` revert for deposits and withdrawals. The recovery recipient remained allowed. Runtime comparison matched the deployed blacklist to the compiled artifact. These results are preserved in [the pre-unpause verification capture](evidence/bridge-recovery-20261016/pre-unpause-verification.json); they are observations made during the response, not newly run publication tests.

The unpause receipt at **02:45:37 UTC**, block **127900807**, is followed by a pinned read-only snapshot at **2026-10-16 02:47:48 UTC**, block **127901098**: `paused = false`, both module registrations and both attacker entries present, recovery wallet not blacklisted, and recovery balance **1015433800** base units. **Nonce 153 was still unused and the original withdrawal getter remained zeroed.** See [post-unpause state](evidence/bridge-recovery-20261016/post-unpause-state.json).

Accordingly, the original claim's payment is blocked by its original recipient being blacklisted, **not by permanent nonce invalidation**. Protection depends on retaining the blacklist entry, its withdrawal-module registration and the bridge's use of this guard. Removing or bypassing that protection can make the outstanding claim payable again. This is a recorded control dependency, not proof of irreversible cancellation or a general bridge-safety assessment.

## What remains outside this recovery

No CL8Y recovery is established by the six response receipts. A captured read-only `transferFrom` attempt made as the actual CL8Y guard reverted with `ERC20InsufficientAllowance`; clearing blacklist storage only in the simulation did not resolve the zero allowance. That failed simulation moved no assets and does not establish a working forced-transfer path. See [the captured simulation](evidence/bridge-recovery-20261016/cl8y-transfer-simulation.json) and [archive/live runtime comparison](evidence/bridge-recovery-20261016/assessment-runtime-comparison.json). CL8Y recovery was deferred by the requester. The separately requested [CL8Y recovery assessment](CL8Y_RECOVERY.md) records the deployed state, the interim compensation decision from the project's CL8Y reserve and the long-term v3 migration recommendation.

The new bridge blacklist is distinct from the historical CL8Y token sending restriction. It does not seize assets already held in external wallets and does not freeze native BNB, Terra bank coins or unrelated external services. No attacker agreement, response to the earlier recovery notice, reimbursement of source fees, recovery of historical BNB/USDT proceeds, or Terra payout is established here. Provider-returned receipts and snapshots remain subject to the evidence limitations in [EVIDENCE.md](EVIDENCE.md).
